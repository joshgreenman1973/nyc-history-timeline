# Fact-check, November 2026

The fifth review covered all 394 cards: every date, title, blurb, detail paragraph, historian's note, reading note and fiction note, checked against outside sources. Eight checkers each took about 50 cards. The same pass rewrote copy that read as machine-written and added inline source links: 521 links now sit on the specific figures and claims they support, on 388 of the 394 cards. All 411 unique link addresses were requested. 393 loaded directly; the other 18 belong to sites that refuse scripted requests (Britannica, Bloomberg, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the Metropolitan Transportation Authority and others), and a sample of those opened normally in a browser.

It corrected 81 errors on 76 cards, including ten from a follow-up on the open questions below. Six were things that had changed since the cards were written.

## Errors corrected

**Colonial era**

- **Jonas Bronck (c. 1639).** Said the Bronx is the only borough named for a person. [Queens is named for Catherine of Braganza](https://www.britannica.com/place/Queens-New-York). The sentence is cut.
- **Treaty of Westminster (1674).** Said the treaty traded New York for Suriname. [The Dutch had held Suriname since 1667 and kept it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Westminster_%281674%29); the 1674 treaty restored conquests.
- **Charter of Liberties (1683).** Said New York joined the Dominion of New England in 1686. [It was added in 1688](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_New_England); the charter was disallowed in 1686.
- **Mill Street Synagogue (1730).** Now "first purpose-built synagogue in continental North America," [as the congregation puts it](https://www.shearithisrael.org/about/our-history/synagogue-buildings/). Earlier synagogues stood in the Caribbean.
- **1741 conspiracy trials.** "About 30 were executed" is now [34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Conspiracy_of_1741).
- **New York tea party (1774).** Said the Nancy had already been sent back to England. [It sailed only after the London's tea was dumped](https://www.frauncestavernmuseum.org/the-new-york-tea-party).
- **Prison ships (c. 1779).** "About a dozen dying nightly" is now "as many as a dozen a day," matching the [Fraunces Tavern Museum's 8 to 12](https://www.frauncestavernmuseum.org/blogs-posts-prisoners-of-the-deep).
- **African Free School (1787).** Dropped "the first school in New York" for Black children. [Elias Neau taught enslaved New Yorkers from 1704](https://trinitychurchnyc.org/stories-news/elias-neau-and-trinitys-outreach-native-americans-and-enslaved).
- **Washington's inauguration (1789).** Congress left Federal Hall [in December 1790](https://www.senate.gov/about/historic-buildings-spaces/past-chambers/federal-hall.htm), about 19 months later, not "barely a year."

**1784–1897**

- **Yellow fever (1798).** The Museum of American Finance gives [a population of about 35,000](https://www.moaf.org/exhibits/pandemics/yellow-fever-1798), not a population "that summer."
- **Gas lamps on Broadway (1825).** May 11, 1825 is [when work on the pipes began](https://todayinsci.com/Events/Technology/GasLightingNewYork.htm), not when it finished.
- **Trinity Church (1846).** [The church was consecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Church_%28Manhattan%29), not "the spire," and it stands at the head of Wall Street, not the foot.
- **Astor Place Riot (1849).** Not "one of the first times American troops killed civilians." Militia [killed rioters in the 1788 Doctors' Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1788_doctors%27_riot).
- **Atlantic cable celebration (1858).** The fireworks that burned City Hall's cupola [were on Aug. 17](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/655617), not at the Sept. 1 jubilee.
- **Cooper Union (1860).** Lincoln's "Brady and the Cooper Institute made me President" is now ["reputedly said"](https://www.mrlincolnandnewyork.org/mr-lincolns-visits/cooper-union-speech/index.html). The remark is secondhand.
- **Draft Riots (1863).** "The deadliest civil disturbance" is now "one of the deadliest." [Estimates for the 1919 Elaine massacre run higher](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/elaine-massacre-of-1919-1102/).
- **The Black Crook (1866).** Retitled from "invents the American musical," a [disputed claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Crook).
- **Pearl Street Station (1882).** Not the world's first central power plant. [Edison's Holborn Viaduct station in London ran eight months earlier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn_Viaduct_power_station).
- **Brooklyn Bridge (1883).** Construction took [13 years, not 14](https://ascemetsection.org/committees/history-and-heritage/landmarks/brooklyn-bridge); work began Jan. 3, 1870.
- **Metropolitan Opera House (1883).** The September pass set opening-night capacity at 3,625. That figure [comes from the house as rebuilt after its 1892 fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera_House_%2839th_Street%29); the 1883 house seated about 3,045. The Age of Innocence note had the opera-box feud backwards: the novel's old families held the Academy of Music boxes.
- **Consolidation vote (1894).** "The Great Mistake" names [consolidation itself](https://www.brownstoner.com/history/brooklyn-history-consolidation-of-new-york-great-mistake/), not opponents' fears.

**1898–1945**

- **American Mutoscope (1895).** Not the first US film studio. [Edison's Black Maria came in 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison%27s_Black_Maria).
- **Brooklyn Museum (1897).** Opened [Oct. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Museum), not Oct. 3.
- **Consolidation (1898).** The referendum passed comfortably overall; only Brooklyn was close, [64,744 to 64,467](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidation_of_New_York_City).
- **Stock Exchange building (1903).** 72 feet is [the trading floor's ceiling height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange_Building); the skylight is 30 by 30 feet.
- **Williamsburg Bridge (1903).** Trolleys [began in 1904 and elevated trains in 1908](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg_Bridge), not at opening.
- **Times Square (1904).** The Times [moved into its tower Jan. 1, 1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Times_Square), not at the renaming.
- **Public Library (1911).** The research collections are [closed-stack](https://www.nypl.org/blog/2016/09/30/magical-mystery-machine-librarians-summon-books-rose-main-reading-room), not open-stack.
- **New York Fed (1914).** First-day deposits were [$100 million from 211 member banks](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/new-york-fed), per the card's own source.
- **1918 influenza, historian's note.** The "50 percent lower peak death rates" finding belongs to [Hatchett, Mecher and Lipsitch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1849868), not to Bootsma and Ferguson.
- **Yankee Stadium (1923).** "First ballpark called a stadium" is now ["one of the first"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium_%281923%29).
- **Martha Graham (1926).** Louis Horst was [Denishawn's musical director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Horst), not its director.
- **Lindbergh parade (1927).** Cut the claim that it set the template for ticker-tape parades; [the first official ones welcomed World War I troops](https://downtownny.com/ticker-tape-parades/).
- **Holland Tunnel (1927).** Not the first car crossing of the Hudson. [The Bear Mountain Bridge opened in 1924](https://nysba.ny.gov/bridge/bear-mountain).
- **1938 charter.** The first Council was elected [entirely by proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_New_York_City_Council_election), not "in part."
- **Benny Goodman (1938).** Not the first big band to headline Carnegie Hall. [James Reese Europe's Clef Club Orchestra played there in 1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reese_Europe).
- **LaGuardia Airport (1939).** It opened as [New York Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaGuardia_Airport), on Flushing Bay, not "North Beach Airport" in the East River.
- **Bebop (c. 1944).** Parker and Gillespie's Three Deuces run [began April 19, 1945](https://plosin.com/milesAhead/Bird/Charlie%20Parker%20Chronology%201945.html).
- **V-J Day (1945).** The Times Square crowd was [about 2 million](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/sites/default/files/2017-07/vj-day-fact-sheet.pdf), not "hundreds of thousands."

**1946–2000**

- **First Tony Awards (1947).** [More than 1,000 guests](https://www.tonyawards.com/history/our-history/), not about 1,200; winners got a scroll and a money clip or compact.
- **Dodgers and Giants (1957).** Ebbets Field became [the privately owned Ebbets Field Apartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebbets_Field), not public housing.
- **Seagram Building.** The date was Dec. 1, 1958, which no source supports. [It was completed in 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagram_Building); the card now gives the year.
- **Ocean Hill-Brownsville, historian's note.** Podair's The Strike That Changed New York came out in 2002, not 2004.
- **Stonewall, reading note.** Carter's 2004 book was not the basis for the monument; [his work on the 1999 National Register nomination came first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_National_Monument).
- **"Ford to City" (1975).** Ford [reversed course Nov. 26](https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/library/document/0248/whpr19751126-004.pdf), not in December.
- **Paradise Garage (1977).** It closed in 1987 [when its lease ran out](https://www.nyclgbtsites.org/site/paradise-garage/), not as Larry Levan's health failed.
- **Son of Sam (1977).** Berkowitz shot [mostly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Berkowitz), not only, couples in parked cars.
- **John Lennon (1980).** The Central Park vigil drew [more than 100,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_John_Lennon), not "tens of thousands."
- **ACT UP (1987).** Larry Kramer's speech was [March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_UP); the group formed March 12.
- **1990s immigration.** The card's own [New York Fed source](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2005/rp050707) gives 1.2 million arrivals against more than 1.3 million departures, so the inflow was not "even larger."
- **Crown Heights (1991).** Yankel Rosenbaum was [a doctoral student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Heights_riot), not a rabbinical student.
- **Amadou Diallo (1999).** The roughly 1,200 civil-disobedience arrests [came in 1999](https://www.democracynow.org/1999/4/1/one_thousand_demonstrators_present_as_diallo), before the trial, not after the 2000 acquittal.

**2001–present**

- **Hearst Tower (2006).** "First green skyscraper" is now [first LEED Gold office tower](https://www.fmlink.com/hearst-tower-certified-as-new-york-city-s-first-green-office-tower/); 4 Times Square (1999) usually gets the broader label.
- **Trans-fat ban (2006).** The federal ban was [issued in 2015](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/final-determination-regarding-partially-hydrogenated-oils-removing-trans-fat) and took effect in 2018.
- **Hurricane Sandy (2012).** "Roughly 43 to 44" deaths is now [44](https://www.nyc.gov/site/cdbgdr/hurricane-sandy/hurricane-sandy.page), per the card's own source.
- **De Blasio (2013).** [First Democrat elected mayor since 1989](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/democrat-bill-de-blasio-elected-mayor-of-new-york), not the first to lead the city since then; Dinkins served through 1993.
- **Officers Ramos and Liu (2014).** The gunman shot his ex-girlfriend [in Owings Mills](https://wtop.com/news/2014/12/police-cop-killer-shot-ex-girlfriend-owings-mills-first/), outside Baltimore.
- **Hamilton (2015).** [The Pulitzer came in April 2016](https://www.npr.org/2016/04/18/474725628/2016-pulitzer-prize-winners-announced), before the Tonys.
- **432 Park Avenue (2015).** It topped out in 2014, so the title now says "completed," and it was the tallest residential building only [until Central Park Tower passed it in 2019](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/09/24/central-park-tower-adrian-smith-gordon-gill-architecture-tops-out/).
- **The Oculus (2016).** [About seven years late](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2016/02/23/no-fanfare-for-4b-wtc-transportation-hubs-opening-in-march), not "nearly a decade."

**Overtaken by time**

- **Second Avenue Subway.** Phase 2 is under construction; [contracts went out in 2024](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-award-182-million-utility-relocation-contract-second-avenue-subway) and 2025.
- **Hudson Yards.** The Vessel [reopened in 2024](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/vessel-reopens-series-suicides-forced-popular-manhattan-tower-close-rcna176407) with mesh barriers.
- **OMNY.** MetroCard sales [ended Dec. 31, 2025](https://www.mta.info/press-release/mta-sunset-metrocard-sales-end-of-year).
- **Eric Adams.** He ran for re-election as an independent and [dropped out in September 2025](https://abc7ny.com/eric-adams-drops-new-york-city-mayoral-race-ends-reelection-campaign-mamdani-cuomo-sliwa/17898741/).
- **Law & Order.** NBC [revived it in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%26_Order_season_21).
- **Mamdani (2025).** "Highest mayoral turnout since 1969" holds only for raw votes; the card now says [more than 2 million votes, the most since 1969](https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2026/04/more-new-yorkers-voted-last-year-any-mayoral-election-1969/413224/).

## Copy edits

About 440 fields were rewritten to remove phrasing that reads as machine-written: "not X but Y" reveals, significance inflation ("watershed," "cemented," "forever changed," "ushered in"), summary closers ("The city would never be the same"), stacked intensifiers and editorial adjectives ("delirious," "lavish"). Facts, quotations and figures were left as they were unless listed above. ## Open questions resolved

The checkers raised 112 flags they could not settle. The ten most likely to need a change were researched afterward, and all ten led to edits:

- **Great Blizzard (1888).** The card gave about 200 deaths in the city as settled. Sources disagree: [History.com says about 200](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/march-11/great-blizzard-of-88-hits-east-coast), Baruch College's NYCdata says 400 and [the Library of Congress says 800](https://guides.loc.gov/chronicling-america-great-blizzard-1888). The blurb now gives the range.
- **RCA Building (1933).** "70-story" is now 66. [The 70 figure came from a hyperbolic press release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rockefeller_Plaza) by Rockefeller Center's publicist; the detail now says so.
- **Wall Street bombing (1920).** "Deadliest act of terrorism on US soil to that point" is now [the deadliest terrorist attack in New York's history until 9/11](https://origins.osu.edu/index.php/history-news/first-wall-street-bomb), as Beverly Gage, the card's own historian, puts it.
- **Rivoli Theatre air conditioning (1925).** Dropped "one of the first buildings cooled for public comfort." Chicago's Central Park Theater [used mechanical air conditioning from 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park_Theater); the card now says so and adds that [more than 300 theaters advertised refrigerated cooling by 1930](https://www.achrnews.com/articles/165867-1925-air-conditioning-comes-to-the-theater).
- **Cross Bronx Expressway (1963).** Cut "evicting an estimated 5,000 families," which no source supports; the blurb already says the highway displaced thousands. The historian's note no longer calls East Tremont "racially integrated," a description that could not be traced to Caro.
- **Dance Theatre of Harlem.** Redated from Aug. 15, 1969 to Feb. 11, 1969, when [the company was incorporated](https://online-exhibitions.library.columbia.edu/exhibits/show/mitchell/dance-theatre-of-harlem--compa/timeline). Aug. 15 was the day the Harlem School of the Arts closed its dance department.
- **Times Square redevelopment (1997).** The historian's lens was Suleiman Osman's book on brownstone Brooklyn, which is about a different subject. It is now Lynne B. Sagalyn's [Times Square Roulette](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/times-square-roulette-remaking-city-icon) (MIT Press, 2001). No other card fit Osman's book, so it was dropped.
- **AT&T Building.** The date was Oct. 1, 1984, which no source supports. The [Landmarks Preservation Commission's designation report](https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2600.pdf) says only that it was completed in 1984; the card now gives the year.
- **Transit strike (2005).** The card stopped at the union board's acceptance of the contract. It now adds that [members rejected it by seven votes](https://labornotes.org/2007/01/union-leadership-re-elected-less-half-vote-ny-transit-workers-get-contract-they-rejected) on Jan. 20, 2006, and that an arbitration panel imposed a contract in December 2006.
- **Chelsea bombing (2016).** "Injures 29" is now "more than 30," the Justice Department's figure in [its sentencing release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/chelsea-bomber-ahmad-khan-rahimi-sentenced-life-prison-executing-september-2016-bombing).

With these, the November pass corrected 81 errors on 76 cards. The other 101 flags are unresolved. The full list, with what each checker found, is in [fact-check-flags-2026-11.md](fact-check-flags-2026-11.md).

# Fact-check, September 2026

An independent pass over all 396 cards then in `data.js` — every date, title, blurb and detail paragraph, plus a machine check of all 835 source links. This is the fourth review the dataset has had; the earlier three are described in `HANDOFF.md`. It found eleven substantive errors, two duplicate events, one structural ordering bug and forty dead links. All of those are fixed below except the dead links, which are fixed for nine cards and catalogued for the rest.

The dataset is now 394 events.

## Errors corrected

**The Frick Collection (Dec. 16, 1935).** Said the museum opened "six years after his widow's death." Adelaide Howard Childs Frick died in October 1931 — four years. Corrected.

**Ford Foundation headquarters.** Dated Dec. 1, 1968. The building opened in 1967 and was inaugurated Dec. 7 that year — the card's own cited source, fordfoundation.org, says 1967. Redated to December 1967.

**RCA Building, Rockefeller Center.** Dated Nov. 1, 1933. The first tenant moved in April 22, 1933; the formal opening was set for May 1 and slipped to mid-May amid the fight over Diego Rivera's lobby mural. Redated to May 1933, with the delay explained in the detail.

**The 1938 city charter.** Blurb said "voters approved" it in 1938. They approved it Nov. 3, 1936; the first Council under it was elected in 1937 and the charter took effect in 1938. Rewritten.

**Bank for Savings (1819).** The card was dated March 16, 1819 and headlined "the city's first savings bank opens," while its own detail said the bank was incorporated in March and opened in July. It opened July 3, 1819. Redated.

**New York City's first COVID-19 case.** Said Feb. 29, 2020. The case was confirmed and announced March 1, 2020, which is what the card's cited Wikipedia source says. Corrected.

**Singer Building demolition.** Claimed it "became the tallest building ever peacefully demolished" in 1968 — true until 270 Park Avenue, at 707 feet, came down in 2021. Updated to name the record's end.

**1798 yellow fever.** Said the epidemic killed "about 2,080 people, roughly six percent of the population." The six percent comes from the cited Museum of American Finance page, which puts the city's population that summer at about 35,000 — a figure that counts only those who had not fled. The 1800 federal census counted 60,515 residents, which would put the toll near three percent. The blurb no longer asserts a percentage, and the detail now shows both denominators.

**Vitascope at Koster & Bial's (April 23, 1896).** Called it "the first commercial projection of motion pictures for a paying American audience." The Latham brothers' Eidoloscope did that on lower Broadway on May 20, 1895, nearly a year earlier. Reframed as the screening that brought projected film into a major vaudeville house and turned it into mass entertainment, with the Eidoloscope noted in the detail.

**Flight 1549 (Jan. 15, 2009).** Detail said the ditching came "less than four minutes after takeoff." Takeoff was 3:25:51 p.m., the ditching 3:30:43 — about five minutes. Corrected.

**Ali-Frazier (March 8, 1971).** Detail said the bout "marked Ali's return three-and-a-half years after his license was revoked." Ali had already returned, against Jerry Quarry, in October 1970. Rewritten.

## Duplicates removed

**The English seizure of New Amsterdam, Sept. 8, 1664,** appeared twice — once at the end of the Dutch era and once at the start of the colonial era, in different words. Because the two cards sat in different eras the build's dedup pass never saw them as a pair. Merged into one card, with both sets of sources kept.

**The Metropolitan Opera House opening, Oct. 22, 1883,** appeared twice, and the two cards disagreed about the house: one said it seated more than 3,600, the other more than 3,000. It seated about 3,625. Merged into one card at the correct figure.

## Ordering bug

Seventy-eight cards displayed out of chronological order. `sortKey` was a bare year, so a year-only event always sorted after every dated event in the same year, and a handful of keys had been bumped by a year to force an order. The Cross Bronx Expressway, whose year an earlier pass corrected from 1960 to 1963, still carried a 1960 key and so appeared before events from 1961 and 1962.

`sortKey` is now `YYYYMMDD`, derived from the display date, with month and day zeroed when the date does not give them. `parts/merge.py` derives it the same way on rebuild, so agent-supplied keys can no longer scramble the order. `app.js` reads years off the new key where it needs them. Inversions are now zero.

## Checked and correct

## Claims left standing with a note

These are contestable rather than wrong, and are flagged here rather than changed:

- **The Nuyorican Poets Cafe, "Oct. 31, 1973."** The founding in Miguel Algarín's apartment is well documented; a precise day is not. The card's confidence label should probably be medium.
- **Collegiate School, c. 1628.** The card already says the year is contested among 1628, 1633 and 1638, and attributes the 1628 claim to the school.
- **MTV "launches from New York," Aug. 1, 1981.** The company and its studios were in New York; the launch feed originated in Fort Lee, New Jersey.
- **The RCA Building's "70 stories."** Sources split between 66 and 70 depending on whether the setback floors are counted. The observation deck is billed as the 70th floor. Left as is.
- **Benny Goodman, "the first time a big band headlined the classical hall."** Carefully worded already; James Reese Europe and W.C. Handy had played Carnegie Hall earlier, but not as a swing band headlining.
- **The 1795 and 1803 yellow fever death tolls (about 730 and about 700).** Both figures are right, but the cited Baruch NYCdata page contains no death figures at all and dates the era's peaks to 1795, 1799 and 1803. The numbers need a source that carries them.

## Method

Every card's date, title and blurb was read in full, and every detail sentence containing a number was extracted and reviewed separately. Claims that could not be settled from the record were verified against primary or institutional sources — the Museum of American Finance, the Ford Foundation, Wikipedia's sourced articles, the New York Fed, the Department of Sanitation, the National WWII Museum, the US House historian's office, the Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat. Link health was checked by requesting all 835 URLs with a browser user agent and a 25-second timeout.
